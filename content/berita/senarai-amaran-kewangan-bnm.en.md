The list is not a complete inventory of every scam in existence. It names entities identified as operating without approval, so a name's absence from it does not mean the entity is approved.

Reading it in reverse is more useful: instead of asking “is this company on the alert list?”, ask “is this company on the licensed list?”. Legitimate financial institutions are licensed, and that licence can be verified.

For securities and digital asset offerings, the Securities Commission Malaysia also publishes lists of licensed parties and investor alerts. Both should be checked before money is sent, not after.
