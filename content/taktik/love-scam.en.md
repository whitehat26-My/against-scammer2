## How it starts

It rarely starts with a request for money. It starts with attention.

Usually someone adds you on Facebook, Instagram or a dating app, or sends a friendly “wrong number” message on WhatsApp. The profile looks well kept: good photos, a convincing career, usually based overseas. Within days the conversation moves to WhatsApp or Telegram, away from the original platform that could suspend their account.

Then come weeks of daily conversation. Good morning messages. Questions about your work. Listening to your problems. There is no request for money at this stage — this is the syndicate investing time, and they are willing to wait.

The first request is usually small and reasonable: phone credit, a document courier fee, a customs charge. Once you have paid a single time, the script changes. Each new problem is bigger than the last, and each has a solid reason why your money will come back shortly.

## Why it works

A love scam does not attack your intelligence. It targets ordinary human needs — to be heard, valued and needed.

Syndicates use scripts tested on thousands of people. They know when to pull away so you feel anxious, when to return warm, and when to introduce a “third party” to confirm the story. By the time you have invested three months of feeling, stopping means admitting those three months were not real. Many people would rather pay once more than face that — and the syndicate knows it.

## How to protect yourself

Run a reverse image search on their profile photos. Most images are stolen from real people's accounts, and a search often finds the original owner.

Ask for a spontaneous video call rather than a scheduled one. Repeated excuses like “my camera is broken” or “the internet on the rig is bad” are part of the script.

Set one rule you never break: no money to anyone you have not met face to face. That rule is easy to follow because it does not require you to judge each story on its own.

If doubt creeps in, tell the whole story to a friend from the beginning. Hearing yourself say it out loud is often enough to make the picture clear.
